challenge: |
  Lumen ran three frontends: the public site, the customer account area and the
  in-store point of sale. Each had its own buttons, its own blue, and its own idea
  of what a form error looked like. Six years of independent decisions had produced
  three products that did not look related.

  The cost was not aesthetic. Every change was implemented three times, and the
  in-store system had drifted far enough that staff training had to cover the
  differences explicitly.
solution: |
  We started with an audit: 41 button variants, 17 blues, four typefaces. Then we
  agreed a single set of tokens, chose one typeface, and rebuilt the twelve
  components that accounted for most of the interface surface.

  The identity refresh was intentionally small. A tightened wordmark, one accent
  colour, and a type scale that works at point-of-sale distances as well as on a
  phone. No rebrand announcement, no new logo story.

  Delivery was incremental. Each team adopted tokens first, then components, one
  release at a time, so nothing had to stop for a big-bang migration.

Design systems fail when they are delivered as a finished artefact and handed
over. This one was adopted in the order that suited each team: tokens first,
because they are cheap to swap in, then components where a rewrite was already
planned.

The audit numbers did most of the persuading. It is difficult to argue for
seventeen blues once somebody has printed all seventeen on one page.
